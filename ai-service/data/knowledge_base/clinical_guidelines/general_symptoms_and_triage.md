# General Symptoms, Triage & Red-Flag Guidance

## 1. Acute Medical Red Flags (Emergency Escalation Required)
The following symptoms require immediate emergency evaluation in a hospital casualty or emergency department:
- **Chest Pain / Tightness:** Crushing substernal pressure radiating to the left arm, jaw, neck, or back, especially accompanied by diaphoresis (sweating), breathlessness, or nausea.
- **Stroke Warning Signs (FAST):** Sudden unilateral facial droop, arm weakness or numbness, slurred speech or inability to formulate words, sudden visual loss.
- **Severe Shortness of Breath:** Inability to complete a sentence without gasping, cyanosis (bluish tint around lips/nails), stridor, or respiratory rate $> 30$ breaths/min.
- **Anaphylaxis:** Sudden lip, tongue, or airway swelling, urticaria, wheezing, and hypotension following food or medication exposure.
- **Severe Head Trauma or Uncontrolled Bleeding:** Direct head trauma with loss of consciousness, persistent vomiting, or profuse arterial hemorrhage.

## 2. Common Mild Outpatient Symptoms
- **Mild Low-Grade Fever ($< 101^\circ\text{F}$):** Ensure adequate oral hydration, rest, and electrolyte replacement. If fever persists $> 3$ days or is accompanied by stiff neck, rash, or shortness of breath, consult a physician promptly.
- **Occasional Tension Headache:** Rest in a quiet, dark environment, ensure hydration, and avoid eye strain. Persistent, worsening, or thunderclap headaches require urgent doctor consultation.
